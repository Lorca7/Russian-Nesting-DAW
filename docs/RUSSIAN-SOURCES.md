# Russian sources behind the scores, synths and samples

This page records the research behind Matryoshka Fugue's Russian material. It covers which traditions fit a fractal fugue, what each built-in score takes from them, and where the facts come from. The scores are original subjects written *in the manner of* these traditions. None of them quotes a copyrighted melody.

## Why these traditions suit a fractal fugue

A fractal fugue needs three things: one idea stated at several scales at once, voices that are copies of each other, and a texture that stays coherent when those copies pile up. Russian music has a lot of all three.

| Tradition | What makes it fractal or fugal | Score |
|---|---|---|
| **Znamenny chant and the Obikhod scale** | The chant moves in small stepwise cells (neumes) that recur at every level of a service. The Obikhod scale is built from repeating tone-tone-semitone groups, so it repeats every fourth instead of every octave. The scale is self-similar by construction. | Znamenny Canon |
| **Troestrochie (three-line chant)** | Three-voice Russian polyphony. A middle voice, the *put'*, carries the chant as a cantus firmus. A *verkh* above and a *niz* below are added to it. The low voice is the most melismatic, and the result is very dissonant by Western standards. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/troyestrochnoye-peniye) · [churchmotherofgod.org](https://churchmotherofgod.org/terminologies/stochny-singing/) · [RISM](https://rism.info/library_collections/2023/03/23/demestvo-early-russian-orthodox-vocal-polyphony.html) | Troestrochie |
| **Rachmaninoff, All-Night Vigil (1915)** | Built on Russian chant. The Nunc dimittis ends with the basses descending to a very low B-flat, which needs *oktavists*. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Night_Vigil_(Rachmaninoff)) · [Yale Choral Artists notes](https://www.artidea.org/sites/default/files/downloads/yalechoralartists.pdf) | Vigil Oktavists |
| **Russian bell ringing (zvon)** | A natural hierarchy of sizes and speeds. In *blagovest*, one great bell is struck in measured strokes. In *perezvon*, each bell is struck in turn from largest to smallest. In *trezvon*, several bells sound together, often three times. In *perebor*, each bell sounds once in turn, then all together. Sources disagree on the direction of the perebor. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_bell_ringing) · [russianbells.com](https://www.russianbells.com/ringing/zvontypes.html) · [churchmotherofgod.org](https://churchmotherofgod.org/articles/bells-and-russian-orthodox-peals/3) | Perezvon Bell Tower, Perebor Funeral Toll |
| **Glinka, *Kamarinskaya* (1848)** | "Changing backgrounds." The folk dance tune is a short ostinato that is repeated, never developed, while the harmony, counterpoint and orchestration around it change. Tchaikovsky regarded it as a model for symphonic use of folk material. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamarinskaya) · [Chandos notes](https://www.chandos.net/chanimages/Booklets/CH9861.pdf) | Kamarinskaya Backgrounds |
| **Folk heterophony (podgoloski)** | Several simultaneous versions of the same melody. Podgolosochnaya polyphony has been called the "trademark" style of Russian folk polyphony, and heterophony is widespread in northern Russia. [polyphony.ge](https://polyphony.ge/?p=558) · [csfu.mrsu.ru](https://csfu.mrsu.ru/en/archives/2384) | Protyazhnaya with Podgoloski |
| **Shostakovich, 24 Preludes and Fugues Op. 87 (1950–51)** | Fugue No. 1 in C major uses only the white keys, with no accidentals. Commentators also note the absence of vertical dissonance in it. [UNT dissertation (Adams)](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc504244/m2/1/high_res_d/1002776039-Adams.pdf) · [piano.or.jp](https://enc.piano.or.jp/en/musics/21822) | White-Key Fugue |
| **The DSCH monogram and Taneyev's counterpoint** | Shostakovich signed his music with D–E♭–C–B. Taneyev's treatise on invertible ("movable") counterpoint (1909) founded the Russian school of counterpoint, and Rachmaninoff, Scriabin and Medtner were among his pupils. [Segall, *Music Theory Online*](https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.14.20.3/mto.14.20.3.segall.php) | DSCH Monogram |
| **Sofia Gubaidulina** | Since the mid-1980s, most of her works have their proportions built on the Fibonacci series, including *Perception* (1983) and *Stimmen… Verstummen…* (1986). She called the golden section "the perfect ratio". [Tsenova, Paul Sacher Stiftung](https://psacher2.mironet.ch/dam/jcr:5865c9a2-08dd-4de0-9cd7-a9c55509ef3d/mitteilungen-pss-14-valeria-tsenova.pdf) · [Kahrs, *MTO*](https://mtosmt.org/ojs/index.php/mto/article/view/490/71) · [Yellow Barn](https://www.yellowbarn.org/node/2122) | Gubaidulina Fibonacci |
| **Stravinsky's Russian period** | Taruskin traces the octatonic collections in *Petrushka* to Stravinsky's Russian inheritance. The texture is static, additive ostinati of varying length that break off and start again. [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/stravinsky-and-the-russian-period/CAA8BE7E910120D22071AEF35F9664FF) | Octatonic Ritual |
| **Scriabin and the ANS** | The mystic (Prometheus) chord. Murzin named his ANS synthesizer after Scriabin. [Wikipedia: ANS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANS_synthesizer) | Scriabin Mystic Bells |
| **Moscow minimalism** | Formed among Moscow conceptualists in the mid-1970s. Martynov combined American minimalist repetition with the repetitive chant of Russian Orthodoxy. Batagov and Karmanov are other key figures. [Wilson, IMTI](https://imti.sias.ru/upload/iblock/717/imti_2021_25_8_27_wilson.pdf) · [Kronos Quartet](https://kronosquartet.org/recordings/detail/music-of-vladimir-martynov/) | Sacred Minimalism |
| **Artemiev and Tarkovsky** | The ANS for *Solaris* (1972). The Moscow electronic music studio opened in 1966, and Artemiev later used its Synthi 100 for *Stalker* (1979). [Wikipedia: Artemyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Artemyev) · [EEFB](https://eefb.org/retrospectives/eduard-artemyevs-compositional-strategies/) | Solaris Photo-Drone |

## Soviet-era sound design (synth presets)

| Era | Instruments and studios | Presets |
|---|---|---|
| 1960s | **Theremin** (termenvox). **ANS**: photoelectronic, sound drawn on glass. **Ekvodin**: Volodin's instrument, heard in Soviet film scores such as *Amphibian Man* (1961). State radio orchestras on tape. [120 Years: Ekvodin](https://120years.net/the-ekvodin-andrei-volodin-russia-1937/) | Termenvox Theremin, ANS Photo-Cluster, Ekvodin Film Lead, Gosteleradio Tape Strings, Sputnik Telemetry |
| 1970s | Transistor combo organs such as the **Junost** ("Vox-style", divide-down). String machines in the estrada and VIA bands. The **Synthi 100** at the Moscow studio. [hollowsun: Vermona](https://hollowsun.com/HS2/products/vermona/index.htm) | Junost Combo Organ, VIA String Ensemble, Synthi Stalker Drone, Solaris Organ Halo, Kosmos Mono Lead |
| 1980s | **Polivoks** (1982–1990). Kuzmin's 12 dB filter uses repurposed op-amps and is famous for its raw self-oscillation. **Aelita** (from 1980, Murom). **Estradin-230 / Altair-231**: 3-VCO monos. Imported FM keyboards such as the DX7 and cheap Elektronika keyboards. [Wikipedia: Polivoks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polivoks) · [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/formanta-polivoks-synthesizer) · [Wikipedia: Aelita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aelita_(synthesizer)) · [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/features/soviet-synths/) | Polivoks Bass, Polivoks Scream Lead, Aelita Brass, Estradin Fat Bass, Estrada FM Piano, Estrada FM Bells, Leningrad Chorus Bass, Elektronika Toy Keys, Perestroika Synth-Pop Pad |

Some of the sound-design features exist to reach these sounds:

- **unison stacks:** ANS clusters, string machines and three-oscillator monos
- **glide:** the theremin
- **hard sync:** 80s leads
- **organ waveform:** combo organs
- **three-phase ensemble:** string machines
- **bit crush:** cheap 80s digital
- **tape age:** a dull top end, saturation and hiss that follows the signal, for how the music reached listeners on tape

## Folk instruments (built-in samples)

All of these are synthesised in code from a physical or spectral model of the instrument. None are recordings.

| Sample | Instrument | Model |
|---|---|---|
| Balalaika pluck, Bass balalaika | Triangular three-string lute; the bass balalaika sits in the ensemble bass. | Karplus-Strong strings with body resonances |
| Domra tremolo | Round-bodied three-string lute, similar to a mandolin, played with tremolo. [balalaika.org](https://www.balalaika.org/bal-instruments.html) | One string re-excited by alternating pick strokes |
| Gusli | Plucked psaltery, often used to accompany tales. [hellomusictheory](https://hellomusictheory.com/learn/russian-instruments/) | Bright pluck with sympathetic open strings |
| Zhaleika | Single-reed hornpipe with a bright, sharp, slightly nasal tone. [hellomusictheory](https://hellomusictheory.com/learn/russian-instruments/) | Odd harmonics, nasal formant, pitch scoop, breath |
| Svirel | Wooden shepherd's flute, one of the oldest Slavic instruments. [organology.net](https://organology.net/?p=1452) | Near-sine tone, tuned breath noise, chiff |
| Bayan, Garmon | Button accordions; the Tula garmon has a brighter, more widely tuned reed sound. | Additive reed banks with detuned (musette) reeds |
| Volynka | Russian bagpipe: chanter plus one or two drones. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volynka) | Buzzy chanter over a drone an octave below |
| Choir 'Ah', Oktavist basso 'O' | Orthodox choir; oktavists are the basso profondo voices that reach the lowest notes. | Formant-filtered additive voices with vibrato |
| Kolokol, Zazvonnye small bells, Blagovest great bell | The bell tower's three sizes. | Inharmonic partials with strike noise and beating |
| Vargan | Jaw harp, an archaic idiophone still used in Russian culture. [atlantis-press](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/125916041) | Decaying buzz through a moving mouth formant |
| Lozhki | Wooden spoons; two to five can be played at once. | Short wooden clacks |
| Buben | Frame drum with jingles, the "Russian tambourine". [mdlbeast](https://mdlbeast.com/xp-feed/music-industry/slavic-sounds-a-guide-to-russian-musical-instruments) | Pitch-falling membrane plus jingle noise |
| Treshchotka | Rattle of wooden boards that imitates hand-clapping. [hellomusictheory](https://hellomusictheory.com/learn/russian-instruments/) | Rapid run of wooden clacks |

## Caveats

- Some sources disagree, and the scores keep both readings where they can: the direction of the perebor, the dates of the ANS, and Polivoks production numbers.
- Instrument attributions for specific Soviet bands (for example Kino or Zodiac) are poorly documented. The 80s presets therefore name a scene and a period ("Leningrad", "Perestroika", "Kosmos") rather than a band.
